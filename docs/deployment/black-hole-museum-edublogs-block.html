The invisible leaves clues

This week we learned that a black hole is a place where gravity becomes so strong that, past a boundary called the event horizon, even light cannot escape.

We learned that scientists cannot see the black hole itself. They look for clues in moving stars, bent light, hot matter outside the event horizon, gravitational waves, and radio observations.

We also learned that a black hole is not a giant space vacuum that pulls in everything nearby. Far away, its gravity acts like the gravity of another object with the same mass.

Essential scientific notes

The famous M87* and Sagittarius A* images were reconstructed from real radio observations. They are not ordinary visible-light photographs, and their orange coloring is a presentation choice.

The visible glow in many black-hole images comes from matter outside the event horizon. The black hole itself remains unseen.

Scientists can measure the surroundings and calculate the boundary, but they cannot receive a message from beyond the event horizon. The physical nature of the deepest interior is not observationally established.
